Asking what an add-on device offered during a visit is meant to do

A device offered during a visit may be described only by a trade name or a short pitch. What the provider called it, where it would be fitted and whether the offer is written down are facts you can state. Photos help. You may be unsure what concern it is meant to address, what showed that concern and what upkeep, replacement parts and running cost it may bring. Mark which is which in your Pembroke request, so a responding provider sees what was proposed and can answer against it.

  1. Return grilles

    Openings that pull room air back into the system.

  2. Return ducts

    The runs leading from each grille to the furnace.

  3. Furnace blower and coil

    Blower assembly, coil and the cabinet around them.

  4. Supply trunk

    The largest duct carrying air away from the furnace.

  5. Branch runs

    The individual runs that split off toward each room.

  6. Supply registers

    The floor, wall or ceiling vents in each room.

Separate runs to ask about:Dryer ventHRV core and screensBathroom fan ductsRange hood exhaust

  • The device and its name

    Write down the name, model and stated purpose exactly as the provider gave them, or photograph any brochure left behind.

  • Where it would be fitted

    Note which duct, vent or unit the device would attach to and who would cut, mount or wire it.

  • Upkeep and what follows

    Ask what replacement parts, cleaning or checks the device needs, and whether any ongoing cost is written into the offer.

Questions About Your Pembroke Request

Would the work be carried out by Pembroke Duct Cleaning, and did it make the offer I received?

The provider who would do the job, not this site, must identify themselves and confirm coverage of your address before anything is arranged. Ask any provider for those details in writing. This is an enquiry website, not a contractor, and it owns no trucks, staff or premises. It cannot have visited your home or made any offer you received. After our enquiry team has reviewed a request, it may be passed to a provider.

Does sending a request about an add-on device commit me to anything?

The first thing a request does is pass your description to our enquiry team for review. It does not approve, order or cancel any device, and it does not tell the provider who visited you what to do. A provider may become involved after that, but any provider, scope and timing are agreed separately with you, and nothing is booked, priced or confirmed by the form. You remain free to decline any offer.

What should I ask about a device a provider offered during a visit?

Ask for its name and model, where exactly it would be fitted, what concern it is meant to address and how that concern was established. Ask what upkeep it needs and whether the offer is in writing. Include those answers in your request so the reviewers can see what was proposed. A short written note from the provider helps.

Can I send a Pembroke request if I only want my ducts cleaned?

Yes. The device question is only one example of what a request can describe. Anyone wanting duct, dryer vent, furnace, HRV or commercial duct cleaning can send the rooms, registers and equipment labels they can see. Our enquiry team reviews the details, and no device or extra item has to be part of the request.

Who can tell me whether a device suits my Pembroke furnace or air handler?

That question belongs with the manufacturer's instructions and an appropriately qualified technician, not with this website. Photograph the equipment label and the filter slot, note the filter size, and ask the provider in writing whether the device was matched to that model. Questions about gas, refrigerant or wiring need appropriately qualified diagnosis. Keep any written reply with the request.

Should a deodorizing unit be agreed before the cause of a smell is found?

Ask what was observed and whether the source of the smell was looked for first. Record where and when the smell appears, and agree any product or device only after the provider has described it, its registered use and the surfaces it touches.

Who can approve a device on a leased commercial system in Pembroke?

The landlord, property manager or condominium corporation may control the air handler or rooftop unit, so a tenant may not be the person who can approve a device. Ask the provider who they expect to sign off, and send those names in your request. Questions about a lease belong with a lawyer or real estate representative.

Can I send a Pembroke request before I decide on the offered device?

Yes. Sending a description early lets you compare the offer with cleaning alone before anything is signed. Include the device name, the place it would be fitted, the stated purpose and any written quote you hold. Our enquiry team reviews the request, and no decision about the offer is made on your behalf. Add anything else the provider said at the visit.
